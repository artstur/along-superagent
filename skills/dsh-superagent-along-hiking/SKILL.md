Read dsh-superagent-along-research first. This skill guides judgment; it is not a fixed list of destinations. For Guangzhou/Shenzhen or another region, use the actual authorized departure city, trip window, desired effort, route preferences and known previous visits. Do not revive demonstration profiles or synthetic history as user facts. If no exact day is decided but weekend choices are delegated, compare reasonable options and state the conditional departure window instead of blocking on an unnecessary question.

Find a small set of feasible routes and compare them. Verify the operator/park/government's current opening and reservation policy, temporary closure notices and dated weather/warnings for the relevant region and trip dates. Prefer official park and meteorological sources. Search for closures as well as positive descriptions. A marketing description or old trip report cannot establish that a trail is open now. A forecast outside its published date range is unknown. An alert relevant to the planned route/time is a weather risk; don't call severe weather a match just because the route is popular.

For each hiking option include both access and weather checks. Mark a closure/unsafe restriction or dangerous weather as risk, unresolved conditions as unknown, and ok only with exact fetched evidence applying to the route/time. Tell the user why an option fits known constraints, what needs checking before departure, and any unknown distance, elevation gain, duration, booking or transport details. Preserve data gaps; a peak's altitude is not the route ascent. Only include precise route facts with exact evidence from a matching route. Do not claim map navigation, real-time location, transport booking or live calendar access unless those tools actually exist.

Use kind:"hiking" in the structured recommendation. Keep 2–3 comparable options when useful; fewer is fine when evidence narrows the result. Exclude closures and unsafe weather. If no safe option is verifiable, explain that and provide a concrete next step or lower-risk alternative without manufacturing a safe forecast. Retrieval and a card do not mean the user went hiking. Complete the due research work with along_task_finish; the separate actual hiking task remains open until the user reports it done.

Official-first workflow and decision rules:
1. Choose at most two plausible candidates from the user's constraints. Before generic travel queries, search `路线名 开放 预约 site:gov.cn` or restrict to an operator domain whose identity is established by a government/official page. Search `路线名 关闭 封闭 森林防火 site:gov.cn` as needed. Do not treat the words “官方” inside a third-party page as proof of its identity. Trip/携程 moments, 本地宝, travel blogs and aggregators may suggest route names, but they cannot establish official current access. If the operator's first-party identity cannot be confirmed, keep access unknown. Never label such a conclusion “已核实开放”. The current host recognizes government publishers and nmc.cn as primary; an unrecognized operator remains conditional.
2. Search official meteorological sites early, with domain restriction (`城市 周末预报 site:nmc.cn` or the relevant official meteorological/government domain), then read the dated forecast. Separately search/read current effective warnings with purpose alerts, for example `城市 当前 生效 气象预警 site:gov.cn`. Ordinary forecast text, “晴/多云/微风”, no rain in a forecast, or failure to find a warning does not prove there is no current warning.
3. In the weather check use alertStatus clear/risk/unknown and alertEvidence exact source quotes. clear requires a fresh fetched primary source explicitly stating no active/current warning, such as “当前无气象灾害预警信号”; a forecast quote is insufficient. An active relevant warning is risk. Without explicit current warning evidence use unknown and say so in summary, reason and nextStep; don't write “无预警/无大风预警” from a forecast. The host will downgrade unsupported access/weather checks and replace contradictory check text. Do not overstate the overall summary or nextStep beyond those checks.
4. Count the whole excursion, including cross-city travel. A route's advertised hiking duration does not prove the user can depart Guangzhou, travel to Shenzhen, hike and return within half a day. If transport has not been verified, leave total journey time unknown, prefer a closer feasible option, or label the cross-city candidate conditional. Do not claim live traffic or tickets. Peak altitude is never cumulative route ascent, and marketing “newbie friendly” is not a verified effort assessment.
